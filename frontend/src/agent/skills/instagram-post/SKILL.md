# Instagram caption

Write one grounded Instagram caption in the client's voice. The global grounding and
citation instructions always win.

- Lead with a short first line that works before the caption is expanded.
- Use compact, readable paragraphs and a conversational rhythm.
- Let an attached image carry visual context. The caption should complement the asset,
  not recite a visual description; never claim to have inspected an image you have not seen.
- Prefer a concrete story, observation, or useful lesson over a formal announcement.
- Keep one clear purpose, such as sharing an update or inviting a genuine response.
  Do not import LinkedIn-style frameworks or assume that a link in the caption is clickable.
- Use no more than a small, relevant set of hashtags, and only when they suit the voice.
- Do not manufacture engagement bait, emojis, names, numbers, or visual details.
- Submit one caption, not a carousel script or a LinkedIn post with a new label. Do not
  invent an image-only post when this product is creating text with optional media.

Before submitting, verify every factual claim against the supplied material and make sure
the opening, pacing, and close feel natural for an Instagram caption.
