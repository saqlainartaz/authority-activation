# LinkedIn post

One post, for one client, in their voice, from their material. This skill covers how the
post is built. What may go in it is settled by the grounding and citing rules in the
instructions, and those win wherever the two documents pull against each other.

Work in three passes. Pick a shape, fit it to the feed, then strip the machine out of it.

## 1. Pick one shape

Choose the single formula that fits the topic and the goal. Never mix two. A post trying to
be a confession and a framework giveaway at once is neither.

F1 Platform-Risk Anaphora. Repeated "X can hurt you" lines, then a reframe and the tactics. Goal: reposts.
F2 R.I.P. Category Obituary. "R.I.P. {category}. Cause of death: {mechanism}", then evidence, then who wins now. Goal: reposts.
F3 Year-over-Year Pivot. "In {last year} I {humble fact}. In {this year} I'm {shift}", then an identity reframe and a mirror question. Goal: comments.
F4 Time-Anchor Confession. "{time} ago, I {vulnerable admission}", then what changed. Goal: comments.
F5 Self-Proving Meta. A post that demonstrates the very thing it claims. Goal: comments.
F7 Odd-Precision Ledger. Oddly precise numbers as receipts. Only real numbers from the material. Goal: saves.
F8 Paid-vs-Free Reversal. "People pay for this. Here it is free", then the framework. Goal: saves and reposts.
F9 Curiosity-Gap Teaser. One strange concrete detail, then the story behind it. Goal: comments.
F10 Contrarian and Receipts. A sacred-cow take, backed by dated evidence. Goal: comments.
F11 Emotional Cold-Open. Drop straight into the hardest moment of a real story. Goal: likes.
F12 Permission Slip. "You're allowed to {relief}", reassurance grounded in experience. Goal: comments.
F13 Bait-and-Switch Reversal. Sounds like bad news, lands as an upgrade. Goal: likes.
F14 Named Gratitude. A tribute naming a real person and what they actually did. Goal: reposts.
F15 Explain-to-Kids. Demystify one piece of jargon in a ten-year-old's words. Goal: saves.
F16 Status-Strip Humility. A senior voice admitting what they still get wrong. Goal: likes.

The formula is a shape, not a script. If the material will not carry the shape you picked,
pick a different one rather than bending the facts to fit.

## 2. Fit it to the feed

- The hook lands inside the first 210 characters, before the "see more" fold.
- Target 900 to 1,300 characters when the material supports it. A shorter grounded post is
  better than padding or refusing useful material.
- Double line breaks between ideas. Never a wall of text.
- Between zero and two hashtags, at the very end, and only if this client's voice uses
  them at all.
- No external links in the body.
- Vary sentence length hard. A three-word sentence next to a twenty-five-word one.
- Use real vulnerability or real stakes when the material supplies them.
- Close naturally. Use a genuine question only when it fits, never as engagement bait.

## 3. Strip the machine out

### Never

**Punctuation and openers.** No em dashes or en dashes anywhere. Use a period or a comma.
No all-caps opener line. No opener that is a rhetorical question.

**These words.** leverage, utilize, facilitate, streamline, robust, seamless, delve,
navigate, unlock, harness, foster, cultivate, fundamentally, essentially, ultimately,
crucially, notably, landscape, ecosystem, paradigm, realm, tapestry, journey, game-changer,
deep dive.

**These phrases.** "in today's fast-paced world". "it's not just X, it's Y". "at the end of
the day". "what do you think?" as the closer. "tag someone who needs this".

**These tells.** A rule-of-three list with no receipts behind it. Perfectly parallel
structure. Sentences that all run the same length. Hedging stacks, where perhaps stacks on
might stacks on could.

### Prefer, and only from the client's material

- A specific number instead of "many" or "massive". Only numbers the material contains.
- At least one named entity, a person or company or place, drawn from the material.
- At least one first-person concrete detail from the client's own story.
- One opinion with stakes, that a reasonable person could argue with.
- A self-correction beat where the material supports one. "I thought this. I was wrong."

These are strong ingredients, not admission requirements. Never refuse viable material merely
because it lacks a number, named entity, vulnerable moment, opinion or self-correction. Pick the
shape that fits what is actually available and write the strongest grounded version of that.

## Before you submit

Read the draft back and check four things: the hook clears the fold, every number and name
is in the material, no banned claim survived, and every factual line about the client has a
citation while no rhetorical line does.
