# LinkedIn post

Write one original personal-profile post for this client. The global grounding, citation,
and safety instructions control what may be claimed; this skill controls presentation.

- Choose one useful idea from the approved material. Start with a specific observation,
  event, lesson, or position when the evidence supports one. Do not invent a confession,
  named person, result, controversy, or vulnerable moment to make a stronger hook.
- For an introduction or promotion request, make the work intelligible to a new reader:
  who or what the business is, whom it serves, what it actually does, and one specific
  reason to care. Include its name when relevant and present in citable material; do
  not substitute the account holder's personal name for an unsupported company name.
  This is an editorial check, not a fixed paragraph template or a demand to name the
  business in every post.
- Give enough context that a reader outside the company can follow the point. Use short,
  natural paragraphs, but vary rhythm rather than splitting every sentence onto a line.
- A lesson, story, update, or concise practical explanation can all work. Do not force a
  formula, contrarian stance, numbered framework, or fixed character target.
- If an image is attached, write text that complements it. Never describe unseen visual
  details or imply the image was inspected unless that capability actually supplied them.
- Use a link, hashtag, emoji, or closing question only when it serves the client's intent
  and voice. Never add an engagement request merely to increase reactions.
- Keep the text native to LinkedIn, but do not assert an algorithmic ranking rule, a
  universal "see more" cutoff, or a guaranteed engagement outcome.

Before submitting, remove generic AI phrasing and unsupported specifics. Check every
factual statement against the supplied material and apply the required claim citations.
